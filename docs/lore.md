# Nightwatch – Lore

The world and story behind the game. Game design lives in [vision.md](vision.md); this document only
covers the fiction and how it is told. "Nightwatch" is a working title.

## Guiding rule: story stays in the background

The game has to be fun even for players who ignore the story completely. The lore is a frame that
explains the mechanics, not something the player has to follow.

- The story is **sprinkled in lightly:** short lines of dialogue, item texts, places that speak for
  themselves, the occasional "echo" on an event hex.
- **No cutscenes** that interrupt play, no walls of text. A villager says one or two sentences at most.
- **Questions may stay open.** Hints are stronger than explanations; the game never has to confirm them.
- The identity of the game comes from **light as a mechanic** (you only see as far as your lantern
  reaches) and from its look, not from a deep backstory.

## The core

The darkness eats the light. You carry a lantern out of the last village, catch the light that monsters
have swallowed, and bring it home. Embers are your currency, your progress and your hope.

## The world

**The darkness** is a blind hunger for light, with no plan and no face. It and its monsters devour
every light they can reach. The further you go from the village, the thicker and hungrier it gets.
Why it is there stays open.

**The Nest** is the fire in the middle of the village. People say it is the nest of the last phoenix.
Nobody has ever seen the bird, and yet the fire never goes out. Some believe the phoenix is still
alive, and that this is the only reason the fire keeps burning. The game never confirms or denies it.
Feathers decorate lanterns and the village crest.

**Watchfires** are old fires scattered across the world. Nobody remembers who first lit them. When you
light one, you give it a spark of your own light and stay bound to it. Its glow reveals the land
around it on the map, but the hexes stay dark in a fight: watchfires give orientation, not protection.
You can rest at a lit watchfire and travel between lit watchfires and the Nest.

## The player

A single, ordinary person without any special gift. You were found in the darkness just outside the
village; nobody knows where you came from, and you only remember it vaguely. The village elder gives
you a lantern carrying a flame from the Nest and sends you out. Not because you are chosen, but
because, being new, you have nothing to lose.

There are no other playable characters. Variety comes from weapons and level-ups (see vision.md).

## Death and waking up

You always wake up in the Nest, like a phoenix rising from the embers. Your lantern carries the
Nest's flame and stays bound to it, so when you fall, the fire takes you back. Nothing else needs
explaining. The villagers greet you when you wake.

A **Phoenix Feather** does the same without dying: it burns up, and you wake in the Nest with
everything you carried.

## Embers

Embers replace gold. Monsters release the light they have eaten, and you catch it in your lantern.
Traders accept embers because only embers keep forges, lamps and potions going. Gold only glitters;
embers burn.

## Villagers

You were found in the dark; now you find others. A few villagers wait at fixed places on the map
(the lost blacksmith in the mine, the herbalist in the mire, a trader far out). Rescuing one brings
them to the village, where they open a new part of the game. They speak in short lines only.

## Seasons: the Long Night

Each season is a new Long Night: the darkness creeps forward again and the watchfires go out. The
village, the people you rescued and what you have built stay.

## Sample lines

Short lines when you wake up or walk past. They can change with progress, e.g. after many deaths or
many lit watchfires.

- Elder: "The Nest leaves no one in the dark. Rest now."
- Elder: "Some say it only sleeps. I say the fire burns. That is enough."
- Herbalist: "You're still glowing a little. Sit by the fire."
- Child: "Have you seen the bird? Someone must have seen it!"
- Blacksmith: "You're alive. That's half the victory."

## Open lore questions

- Is the phoenix still alive, and does it have something to do with the Old Tower?
- Is the darkness only hunger, or does it have a will?
- What lies inside the Old Tower, and why is its gate sealed?
- The final title of the game.
